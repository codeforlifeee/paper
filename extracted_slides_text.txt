Introduction - Remote Sensing
Ph.D. Progress Report Presentation 25ᵗʰ June 2026

3 
Remote Sensing: Acquiring data about targets
from a distance without physical contact [1].
Platforms: Utilizes sensors on satellites, aircraft,
or drones [2].
How It Works: 
‣ Energy: The Sun or active emitters provide
radiation. 
‣ Interaction: Materials uniquely absorb, reflect, or
transmit this energy. 
‣ Detection: Sensors capture the backscattered or
emitted signals. 
‣ Processing: Algorithms classify and interpret the
resulting images.
Figure 1: Fundamental of Remote Sensing

Hyperspectral Imaging (HSI)
Ph.D. Progress Report Presentation 25ᵗʰ June 2026  
                 Information Technology 
4 
Definition: Captures electromagnetic energy across hundreds of narrow, contiguous spectral bands.
Output: Forms a 3D data cube (X,Y, λ), containing spatial and spectral dimensions.
Advantage: Provides a unique "spectral fingerprint" to identify visually identical materials. 
Figure 2: HSI principles diagram (Image credit: Lincoln Laboratory Journal, 4)

=== PAGE 5 ===
Working Principles of HSI
Interaction: Materials absorb and reflect specific wavelengths based on chemical composition. 
Dispersion: Optical sensors and prisms split incoming light into discrete bands. 
Data Cube Formation: Stacks 2D spatial images taken at distinct wavelengths into a single volume
Hyperspectral Cube (Data cube or Hypercube): A 3D data structure where each pixel has a complete spectral
signature across all bands [4].
Structure: X & Y-axis: Spatial dimensions (image width & height). Z-axis: Spectral dimension (Bands/Channel).

Research Gaps
Ph.D. Progress Report Presentation 25ᵗʰ June 2026  
                 Information Technology 
9 
Most methods rely on predefined superpixels whose quality strongly affects performance.
Existing approaches often focus on either spectral or spatial information and do not fully exploit their
joint relationships.
Many superpixel methods emphasize local neighborhood information while ignoring long-range
dependencies and global scene structure.
Superpixels may contain heterogeneous materials, causing feature distortion and reduced
classification accuracy.
Most supervised superpixel methods still require labeled samples and struggle in small-sample
scenarios.

Research Objectives 
Noise & Redundancy Reduction: Employs SRAM for adaptive band
weighting to filter noise and CSBA using α-probabilities to prevent feature
duplication.
Calibration & Class Balance: Integrates SCFG with covariance outer
products for dynamic channel calibration while utilizing a Joint Spread-
Focal Loss to ensure accuracy on minority classes.

Techniques Employed in SGSCF-Net 
Ph.D. Progress Report Presentation 25ᵗʰ June 2026  
                 Information Technology 
                       10 
SRAM: Attenuates atmospheric
noise using spectral-band attention
before convolution.
CSBA: Fuses complementary
branches to learn non-overlapping
spectral features.
SCFG: Uses covariance-based
calibration, eliminating dense FC
layers and reducing parameters by
85%.
Spread-Focal Loss: Increases
minority-class feature margins and
prevents feature collapse.

Experimental Plan & Sensitivity Sweeps

Environment: PyTorch on NVIDIA Tesla-2 GPU, 200 epochs, Adam optimizer. 
Patch Size Sweep: Planning to Test on 9x9, 11x11, 13x13, 15x15, 17x17. (
maximized context without boundary noise). 
Batch Size Sweep: Planning to Test on 32, 64, 128, 256. (Smaller batches

ratios (0.2%, 1%, 5%, 10%).
